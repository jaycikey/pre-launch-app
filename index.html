One account, on purpose

You don't have five balances.
You have one you can spend.

Aggregators show you every account and still leave the real question open: what can I actually spend today?

The day it leaves, not the day you buy

A card purchase on the 5th that you pay off on the 28th lands on the 28th — because that's when the money actually goes.

Cards and savings are outflows, not ledgers

A card payment or a transfer to savings is one line on the timeline. Nothing to reconcile, nothing to keep in sync.

Paid by someone else? Marked and excluded

Anything that never touches your account stays in your history but out of the forecast, so the number you see is the number you have.

Money you're owed is watched too

A bill you forget to tick makes the forecast pessimistic — you have more than it says. A payment that never arrives does the opposite: the app has already counted it, and now it's promising money you don't have. So income is tracked the same way bills are.

A limit we chose

This is a limit we chose and intend to keep. The moment an app starts asking what's on each card, it stops answering the only question that matters.

No bank connection required. No ads, no selling data — your numbers stay in your hands. Optional encrypted sync if you want more than one device.
